Wood and plastic, reborn.

We turn sawdust, wood waste and natural fibres, together with waste polypropylene, into durable wood-plastic composite (WPC) products, using a low-water process.

What we makeContact us
The problem

Two kinds of waste that rarely get a second life

Wood waste

Sawmills and furniture units produce large amounts of sawdust, offcuts and fibre. Most of it is burnt or dumped.

Waste polypropylene

Used PP packaging, sacks and containers often end up in landfill or open burning instead of being recycled into something useful.

What we make

Wood-plastic composites from waste

Lignio combines wood flour and natural fibres with recycled polypropylene to make WPC: a material that looks and works like wood, without cutting a single tree for it.

Made from waste

Both main inputs, wood waste and waste PP, are by-products that would otherwise be burnt or landfilled.

Low-water process

Our dry compounding and forming route uses very little water compared with conventional wood-panel processing.

Built for Indian weather

WPC handles moisture better than plain wood, which makes it a good fit for humid regions like Assam and Kerala.

Where it can be used

Decking & flooringWall & ceiling panelsFurniture partsFencing & railingsDoor & window framesGarden products
How it works

From waste to board in four steps

  1. Collect

    Sawdust, wood waste and fibres from local mills; post-use PP from scrap collectors.

  2. Prepare

    Wood is dried and sized into fine flour. PP is cleaned, sorted and shredded.

  3. Compound

    Wood flour and PP are blended with small amounts of processing aids into a uniform composite.

  4. Form

    The composite is shaped into boards, panels and profiles, then cooled and finished.

Why it matters

A small circular economy, close to the source

Less burning, less landfill

Every board keeps wood waste and plastic out of open fires and dumps.

Saves timber

WPC can replace solid wood in many everyday uses, easing pressure on forests.

Local value

Sourcing waste from nearby mills and scrap collectors creates income from material that was thrown away.
